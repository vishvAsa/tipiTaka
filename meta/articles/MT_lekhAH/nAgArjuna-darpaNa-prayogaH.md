I saw Halloween celebrants observe a vestige of occidental catoptromancy. Even under the heavy hand of Abrahamism, it was practiced by the English Renaissance magician John Dee. Dee's practice has parallels to that taught by the siddha Nāgārjuna. One example of Nāgārjuna's teaching of darpaṇa-prayoga is restored from a corrupt manuscript:

atha darpaṇe rūpa-darśanam ||
rakta-haya-māra-kṛt kusumam iti |
rakta-karavīra-puṣpaṃ bhallātakam-amlā-vetasam ebhir darpaṇaṃ sammārjya saṃśodhya aśva-gardabhoṣṭra-rūpaṃ paśyet || 13 ||
rakta-haya-māra-kusumaṃ bhallātakam-amlā-vetasam ebhiḥ |
turaga-khara-dīrghakandhara-rūpaṃ sammārjya-darpaṇe paśyet || 13 ||
haya-go-varāha-vānareti sugamam || 14 ||
haya-go-varāha-vānara-kharoṣṭraṃ teṣāṃ nakhaiḥ puṭāgninā dagdhaiḥ |
dardura-vasā-miśrais teṣāṃ rūpāṇi pūrvavat paśyet || 14 ||
aṅkola-taila-kajjaleti | aṅkola-vṛkṣa-bīja-tailena dīpaṃ kṛtvā kajjalaṃ gṛhītvā gavāṃ ghṛtenāloḍya tena kajjallena dṛṣṭim añjayet |
puṣya-nakṣatre'ñjita-nayano janmāntare yāni rūpāntarāṇyāsan tāni darpaṇe paśyati || 15 ||
aṅkola-taila-surabhi-kṣīrair dṛgañjanaṃ puṣye |
paśyati darpaṇa-madhye rūpāṇi bhavāntare yāni || 15 ||
añjitanayaneti | tagara-phalaṃ cūrṇaṃ kṛtvā aṅkola-tailenāloḍya cakṣuṣī añjayet |
divyākṛtiṃ puruṣaṃ paśyati | punaḥ kevala-maṣī-añjanāt prakṛtiṃ vrajati | divyaṃ puruṣaṃ na paśyatītyarthaḥ || 16 ||

Now begins the manifestation of forms in a mirror:
[The commentary explains the phrase] "the blossoms of red oleander" (rakta-hayamāra-kṛt-kusumam): Having rubbed and purified the mirror with these: red oleander flowers (rakta-karavīra), marking nut (bhallātaka), and wild sorrel (amlāvetasa); one sees within it the form of a horse, a donkey, or a camel. (13)
[Mūla:] Having cleansed the mirror with these: red oleander blossoms (rakta-hayamāra), marking nut, and wild sorrel; one beholds the forms of a horse (turaga), a donkey (khara), and a long-necked camel (dīrgha-kandhara). (13)
[The commentary notes:] The phrase beginning with "horse, cow, boar, monkey" (haya-go-varāha-vānara) is easily understood. (14)
[Mūla:] By utilizing the hooves and claws of a horse, cow, boar, monkey, donkey, or camel, incinerated in a sealed crucible, and mixed with the fat of a frog, one beholds their respective forms reflected [in the mirror] just as before. (14)
[The commentary explains the phrase] "the collyrium [prepared with] Aṅkola oil": Having fashioned a lamp with the seed-oil of the Aṅkola tree, one collects its soot. Having stirred this soot with cow’s ghee, one should anoint one's eyes with it. A practitioner whose eyes are anointed under the asterism of Puṣya beholds in the mirror whatever different bodily forms they assumed in their previous incarnations. (15)
[Mūla:] When the eye-collyrium prepared with Aṅkola-oil and cow's milk/ghee is applied under the Puṣya asterism: one beholds directly in the center of the mirror the forms of oneself in previous existences. (15)
[The commentary explains the phrase] "having anointed the eyes" (añjita-nayana): Having ground the fruit/root of the Tagara plant into a fine powder and stirred it with Aṅkola oil, one should anoint both eyes.
[By doing so], one beholds a being of divine form (divyākṛtiṃ puruṣam). Furthermore, by subsequently applying a plain collyrium (kevala-maṣī-añjana) without any herbal admixtures, one returns to one's normal state, meaning, one ceases to see the divine being. (16)

One may wonder why one would want to see visions of various animals. One possibility suggested by a catoptromancer was that it is a practice run. Once you are able to master it, you can move on to successfully seeing your prior incarnations. Finally, you can graduate to seeing divya-puruṣa-s and conversing with them.
